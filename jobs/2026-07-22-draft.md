## Post copy

In 2025, something flipped: for the first time, brands built into games more often than they built standalone worlds around games. I think that's the whole ballgame.

For a few years, the playbook was: build a branded world, throw a launch party, post the concurrent-player screenshot, move on. Fun for a press release. Bad for a marketing budget.

The data backs up what we've been seeing on the ground — 88% of brand activations in virtual worlds are happening on just two platforms, Roblox and Fortnite, and the average brand ran 1.8 activations in 2025, up from 1.4 in 2023. Brands aren't dropping in once anymore. They're building presence.

HI-CHEW is running mini-games inside Fortnite. PUMA has Manchester City kits live across multiple games. CeraVe built an obstacle course into Roblox. None of these are one-off stunts — they're integrated into how people already play.

We've seen the same pattern up close: Diesel/OTB ran a campaign with us on PLAY3, then came back for a second one. That's the number I care about more than any single launch metric — repeat spend is the only stat that tells you something actually worked.

As Amy Zehren, CMO of Basket Entertainment, put it: "Players don't want to visit an ad, they want something that enhances how they already play." That's basically the whole lesson in one line. The brands getting it right in 2026 are treating virtual worlds like a channel, not a stunt.
